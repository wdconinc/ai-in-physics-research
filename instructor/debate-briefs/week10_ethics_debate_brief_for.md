# Week 10 Ethics Debate Brief (FOR)
## Motion
**AI-assisted text in research papers should require caption-level disclosure at each affected figure/table/section.**

## Position
Support the motion: local, caption-level disclosure improves traceability, reviewer trust, and post-publication accountability.

## Core arguments
1. **Reader-level transparency**: disclosures embedded where claims appear reduce ambiguity versus a single end-of-paper statement.
2. **Reproducibility**: section-local disclosure links claims to tool usage and model versioning.
3. **Error accountability**: if an AI-assisted segment is flawed, the affected scope is immediately visible.
4. **Norm alignment**: major publishers increasingly require explicit AI-use reporting in manuscripts.

## Evidence prompts for class use
- APS-style authorship and integrity expectations emphasize responsibility for all manuscript content.
- Nature editorial guidance expects transparent reporting of AI/tool assistance.
- Reproducibility best practice: provenance should be closest to the claim, not buried in acknowledgements.

## Anticipated counterarguments and rebuttals
- **Counter**: “Too much disclosure clutter.”
  - **Rebuttal**: concise one-line captions are lightweight and reduce audit cost later.
- **Counter**: “A single ethics section is enough.”
  - **Rebuttal**: global disclosures hide which exact claims/tools are linked.

## Cross-exam questions
1. If not caption-level, how do reviewers map a contested claim to AI-assisted drafting?
2. What is the minimal disclosure that still supports independent replication?
3. Why should readers infer provenance from one generic statement?

## Closing line
Caption-level disclosure is a small burden that creates large gains in trust, auditability, and scientific integrity.
