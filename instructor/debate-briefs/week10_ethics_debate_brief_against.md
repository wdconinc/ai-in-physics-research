# Week 10 Ethics Debate Brief (AGAINST)
## Motion
**AI-assisted text in research papers should require caption-level disclosure at each affected figure/table/section.**

## Position
Oppose the motion: mandatory caption-level disclosure is overly rigid; robust manuscript-level disclosure can preserve transparency with less noise.

## Core arguments
1. **Signal-to-noise**: repeated micro-disclosures can obscure scientific content and burden readers.
2. **Policy proportionality**: risk varies by use case; a single detailed methods/ethics section can be sufficient.
3. **Author workflow realism**: granular tracking of minor language edits is costly and inconsistently applied.
4. **Review practicality**: reviewers need accurate provenance summaries, not repetitive labels.

## Evidence prompts for class use
- APS/Nature-style policies focus on author responsibility and transparency, but do not always mandate per-caption format.
- Reproducibility hinges on methods/data/code traceability more than wording-level annotation granularity.
- Overly strict formatting rules can incentivize superficial compliance.

## Anticipated counterarguments and rebuttals
- **Counter**: “Without caption-level labels, provenance is hidden.”
  - **Rebuttal**: require a structured AI-use appendix mapping tools to manuscript sections.
- **Counter**: “Granularity improves accountability.”
  - **Rebuttal**: accountability is stronger with enforceable audits than with repeated boilerplate.

## Cross-exam questions
1. Which failures are prevented only by caption-level labels and not by structured section-level disclosure?
2. How do we keep disclosure useful rather than performative?
3. What is the faculty/reviewer workload impact of strict per-caption enforcement?

## Closing line
Strong, structured manuscript-level disclosure delivers transparency without sacrificing readability or creating compliance theater.
