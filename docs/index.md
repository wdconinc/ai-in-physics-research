# Generative AI in Physics Research
## Generative AI & Knowledge Platforms

A 14-week graduate seminar for physics PhD students covering practical workflows for
generative AI, retrieval-augmented generation, the Model Context Protocol, and agentic
pipelines — with every technique grounded in real physics problems.

---

## Course at a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | Seminar / workshop — no traditional lectures |
| **Enrollment** | Maximum 10 graduate students |
| **Contact hours** | Twice a week for 1 hour 15 minutes |
| **Course dates** | September 9 to December 11, 2026 |
| **Method of evaluation** | Mini-assignments, proposal pitch, peer review, final paper & showcase |

### Three Phases

=== "Phase 1 — Common Baseline (Weeks 1–5)"
    Build shared technical vocabulary. Every student implements the same GenAI and MCP
    pipelines on provided datasets before diverging into independent projects.

    - Week 1: LLM architecture and responsible use
    - Week 2: AI-assisted coding and its failure modes
    - Week 3: Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
    - Week 4: Tool use and the Model Context Protocol
    - Week 5: GenAI in theoretical physics + **project proposal pitches**

=== "Phase 2 — Project Ideation (Weeks 6–7)"
    Each student begins building infrastructure for their independent project.

    - Week 6: Building production-quality MCP servers
    - Week 7: Evaluation frameworks for AI in physics

=== "Phase 3 — Execution & Polish (Weeks 8–14)"
    Students execute their projects with coaching support from the instructor.

    - Weeks 8–9: Pipeline implementation and debugging
    - Week 10: Ethics, reproducibility, and peer code review
    - Weeks 11–12: Reading groups and second round of peer review
    - Week 13: Scientific writing and practice presentations
    - Week 14: **Departmental showcase** and course retrospective
